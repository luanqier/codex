# Performance Direction

## Embody emotion through action

Never direct a character with an emotion label alone. Convert the internal state into visible, playable behavior that follows from the scene trigger and the character's established personality.

Build a performance beat from the smallest useful subset of:

1. Trigger and reaction delay.
2. Gaze target, focus change, avoidance, or sustained eye contact.
3. One micro-expression change in brows, eyelids, mouth, jaw, or facial tension.
4. Breath rhythm, swallow, voice onset, pause, or interrupted speech.
5. Hand behavior, object handling, shoulder state, torso angle, weight shift, or foot placement.
6. Distance and orientation toward another character.
7. Action tempo and the final settled pose.

Use two to four strong cues per emotional change. Do not enumerate every facial muscle and limb in every shot.

## Causal performance formula

Write `动作` in this order when applicable:

```text
触发 → 半拍反应 → 微表情或目光变化 → 身体/手部动作 → 空间距离或节奏变化 → 落点
```

Examples:

- Instead of `高兴`, write: `听到回应后停顿半拍，肩线松开，眼尾微弯，向对方靠近半步，最后把手中的令牌稳稳收好。`
- Instead of `紧张`, write: `目光先扫向门口，呼吸短暂停住，指腹沿杯沿轻轻收紧，身体重心向后移，听见脚步远去后才缓慢放松。`
- Instead of `压住不满`, write: `下颌短暂收紧，视线从对方脸上移向桌面，抬手把卷轴推回原位，语速放慢，句尾停顿更重。`
- Instead of `悲伤`, write: `听完后没有立刻回应，目光落在空椅上，手指停在衣袖边缘，呼吸逐渐放缓，转身时刻意避开众人的视线。`

## Character-specific acting

- Derive performance from the character lock record, voice profile, relationship, status, current objective, and prior shot state.
- Let different characters express the same emotion differently. A restrained strategist may reduce movement and lengthen pauses; an impulsive youth may step forward, speak sooner, and reveal more hand movement.
- Preserve signature movement habits without repeating the same gesture in every shot.
- Use props and spatial relationships as performance tools only when they already exist in the scene.
- Keep gestures physically possible and compatible with costume, held objects, injuries already safely adapted, camera framing, and available screen time.

## Subtext and dialogue

- Let action reveal what dialogue does not state directly.
- Align pauses, breath, gaze, and object handling with the voice profile and spoken line.
- Avoid making every line accompanied by a gesture. Use stillness when it carries more pressure.
- Do not duplicate the dialogue's literal meaning with an obvious gesture unless clarity requires it.

## Continuity and economy

- Carry the final gaze, posture, hand state, held object, distance, and emotional pressure into the next shot unless a visible change occurs.
- Write only what changes from the established baseline. Inherit neutral posture, costume, voice, and movement habits from the character bible.
- Avoid stock repetition such as constant brow movement, clenched hands, tears, shouting, gasping, or dramatic turning.
- Keep background-character acting simpler than focal-character acting so the frame remains readable.

## Performance QA

Reject or rewrite when:

- `动作` contains only an emotion label or abstract mental state;
- the behavior has no visible trigger or plausible motivation;
- facial expression, breath, hands, posture, and dialogue contradict one another without intentional subtext;
- the gesture cannot fit the shot duration or camera framing;
- a character resets posture, gaze, object state, or distance between shots without a transition;
- multiple characters use the same generic performance pattern;
- the description lists excessive micro-actions that compete with the primary story action.
